Life is filled with moments of boredom.
Minutes are spent waiting for the bus, doctor, or microwave.
If you fill these moments of boredom with short form video,
two things can happen.
First, you lose resilience against boredom.
If you don't practice tolerating boredom,
boredom becomes intolerable.
Second, you build a habit of opening a video feed
whenever you get bored.

These two effects combine disastrously
when a person affected by them tries to be productive.
Most work is at some point boring.
When a person sensitized to boredom inevitably hits a boring stretch,
they seek immediate reprieve.
They open their video feed of choice,
and are blasted with a fire hose
of the most engaging content humanity has ever created,
curated just for them
by an algorithm that optimizes for a singular goal: total watch time.[^time]
The person has gone from engagement withdrawal
to the highest level of engagement currently manufacturable.

A short-form video feed has no built-in stopping points.
It has no friction.
Like a body in motion, the user continues in a straight line
until acted upon by an external force.
Once a person has been swept away,
they cannot escape by themselves.
They have to be pulled out.[^notme]

It's not a problem 
when you're going to be interrupted 
by the eventual bus, doctor, etc.
Just be aware that building the habit is potentially disastrous.

If you have boredom weakness and a distraction-escape habit,
you can fix them by ~~buying my course~~
by thinking about what caused them in the first place. 
Build your tolerance to boredom by spending those short moments bored.
Limit your consumption of ultra-processed media[^food].
You're not missing out; the content lacked substance anyway.
Identify what triggers the habit and redirect[^redirect] it to trigger something healthier,
like getting up and stretching.

At least, I hope these strategies work,
because they are the ones I am implementing,
and I'm still at the point where I opened {{<referrer>}} a dozen times while writing this post.

[^time]: YouTube started recommending videos by watch time in 2012.
Selecting only by watch time is not without side effects.
Since 2012, YouTube has had to add adjustments
to prevent the watch time metric from promoting
sensationalistic tabloid content,
hate-watching,
"video of minors in risky situations",
fake news,
unfairness across protected groups,
"borderline content",
and AI slop.
Although, it could be that any recommendation algorithm is prone to those issues.


[^notme]: If this is not an accurate description of your own experience,
then I must not be describing you.

[^food]: Short form video is quite like ultra-processed foods:
mimicking what humans have traditionally consumed,
but engineered by scientists to be hyper-palatable,
in the process loosing all substance.

[^redirect]: Consider using a browser extension to literally redirect
to something like https://screaming.saej.in/?q=GET+UP+AND+STRETCH
